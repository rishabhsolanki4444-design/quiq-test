India gets nearly 300 sunny days a year. So why do 7 in 10 Indians have **vitamin D deficiency**? The answer lies in how most of us actually live: inside offices, behind glass windows, under sunscreen, and covered for cultural reasons. Darker skin also requires significantly more sun exposure to produce the same amount of vitamin D as lighter skin. The result is a silent epidemic hiding in plain sight, across every city, income level, and age group in the country.

## What Exactly Is Vitamin D Deficiency?

Vitamin D is a hormone your body produces when skin is exposed to UVB rays from sunlight. It controls how your body absorbs calcium, supports bone strength, regulates immunity, and affects muscle function. Doctors measure it through a blood test called the **serum 25(OH)D test**. A reading below 20 ng/mL is classified as deficient. A review published in the Journal of Family Medicine and Primary Care found that **vitamin D deficiency in India** affects between 40 and 99 percent of the population, with most studies reporting a prevalence of 80 to 90 percent across all age groups. <sup><a href="#ref-1" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(1)</a></sup>

## 5 Signs You Might Be Deficient

These **low vitamin D symptoms** rarely announce themselves clearly. They build slowly and are easy to dismiss as the cost of a busy life.

1. **Constant fatigue** that sleep does not fix is one of the earliest and most common signs. According to StatPearls, published by the National Institutes of Health, even mild and chronic vitamin D deficiency can cause myalgia, fatigue, and muscle weakness, symptoms that are often mistaken for general tiredness or overwork. <sup><a href="#ref-2" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(2)</a></sup>
2. **Bone pain and lower back pain** that feel dull and persistent, particularly in the hips, lower spine, and legs, are a classic indicator. A peer-reviewed study in the Journal of Musculoskeletal Medicine confirms that low vitamin D levels lead directly to bone pain, low bone mass, and increased fracture risk, all of which begin silently before any visible damage occurs. <sup><a href="#ref-3" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(3)</a></sup>
3. **Muscle weakness or cramps** without a clear physical cause are another warning sign. Research published in PMC by the National Institutes of Health found that proximal myopathy, a waddling gait, and muscle weakness are well-established clinical features of chronic vitamin D deficiency, particularly in adults with levels below 20 nmol/L. <sup><a href="#ref-4" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(4)</a></sup>
4. **Frequent colds, infections, or low immunity** may also point to insufficient vitamin D. A case-control study from Kasturba Medical College found that 54 percent of patients with newly diagnosed pulmonary tuberculosis had vitamin D deficiency, compared to only 26 percent of healthy controls, underlining the direct link between low vitamin D and weakened immune defence. <sup><a href="#ref-5" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(5)</a></sup>
5. **Mood changes, low motivation, or persistent sadness** round out the five. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 53,235 participants published in a peer-reviewed journal found that vitamin D supplementation had a significant positive effect on depressive symptoms. **Vitamin D deficiency symptoms in women** in particular often include emotional flatness and low motivation. <sup><a href="#ref-6" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(6)</a></sup>

## Who Is Most at Risk in India

While vitamin D deficiency in India affects nearly everyone, some groups face the highest risk. **Pregnant and lactating women** are among the most vulnerable, with studies showing over 96 percent are deficient and their newborns inherit the same low levels. A population-based study of 1,403 adults in Delhi found that urban women had significantly higher deficiency rates than men, with female gender independently associated with severe deficiency. <sup><a href="#ref-7" className="text-blue-400 hover:underline">(7)</a></sup> Adolescent girls, office workers, vegetarians, and adults over 50 are equally at risk across all regions of the country.

## Why Do So Many Indians Stay Deficient Without Knowing

The symptoms, fatigue, mild aches, low mood, are easy to blame on stress or poor sleep. Most people never think to test. And when they do, a **vitamin D blood test** at a private lab costs anywhere between Rs 450 and Rs 2,500 depending on the city and provider, a price barrier that stops most Indians from ever finding out. The deficiency stays hidden, and the damage quietly builds.
